Living in Uji

The River That Runs Through Everything

"The boats moved slowly downstream in the dark, the kagaribi casting orange light on the water and the hills beyond."

Before Uji was known for matcha, before it was known for The Tale of Genji, it was known for its river. The Uji River runs fast and cold out of Lake Biwa, cutting through a narrow valley before widening at the town. I live close enough to hear it on quiet nights.

Cormorant Fishing (Ukai)

Cormorant fishing (ukai) is one of Japan's oldest fishing traditions — documented for over 1,300 years. A trained cormorant dives for sweetfish (ayu) in the river at night, guided by a master fisherman (usho) who controls the bird with a leash.

The birds work in the dark, lit only by the flame of an iron basket (kagaribi) suspended over the bow of the boat. The fire attracts the fish to the surface. It is, by any measure, an extraordinary thing to watch.

The 2026 Centennial Revival

Uji's ukai tradition was suspended for many decades. In 2026 — marking the centennial of its last regular practice — cormorant fishing has been revived on the Uji River. This is not a reconstruction for tourists; it is a restoration of a practice that was genuinely part of life here for centuries.

Practical Information

  • Season: Summer (typically July–September)
  • Viewing: From the riverbank or from a chartered viewing boat
  • Booking: Viewing boat tickets sell out quickly. Contact Uji City Tourism Association.
  • Note: Arrive early to secure a good spot on the bank.
