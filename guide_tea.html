Uji Tea

Uji Tea: Why This Cup Is Different

"When you taste proper Uji matcha, ground fresh and whisked in a bowl, the difference is not subtle."

Every café in Kyoto serves matcha. By the time most visitors reach Uji, they assume they already know what matcha tastes like. They don't. Not yet.

Uji has been producing green tea since the 13th century. The combination of river mist, temperature variation, and the particular soil of this valley creates a tea with a depth of flavour — a rich, almost oceanic umami — that you cannot replicate elsewhere.

Where to Go

Tsuen (通圓): Established 1160, making it one of the oldest tea houses in Japan. Sit by the window facing Uji Bridge and the river. This is not a performance for tourists — it is simply how things have been done here for centuries.

Nakamura Tokichi Honten (中村藤吉本店): The most celebrated tea house in Uji, in a beautifully preserved Meiji-era townhouse. The matcha parfait has become the defining image of Uji food. It lives up to its reputation.

Local Advice

At some point, buy a tin of proper loose-leaf Uji gyokuro and make it at home. Brew it cooler than you think (around 50–60°C), steep shorter than you think (about 90 seconds). The flavour will be unlike anything you have tasted from a tea bag.

Practical Information

  • Nakamura Tokichi: Book online where possible (walk-in queue can be 45–60 min)
  • Tsuen: Accepts walk-ins; rarely crowded on weekday mornings
  • Shopping: Byodoin Omotesando is lined with established tea shops.
  • Payment: IC cards (Suica/ICOCA) are accepted at most shops.
